Sheldon Natenberg — Option Volatility & Pricing (2nd Ed.)

The desk's canonical reference for options theory. This page distills the 2nd edition (1994/2015) into actionable concepts. Not a summary — a working cheat sheet for the desk. Scribd link: original PDF (paywalled).

Index terms — implied volatility, historical volatility, volatility smile/skew, Black-Scholes, binomial model, Greeks (delta, gamma, theta, vega, rho), volatility trading, delta-neutral, risk reversal, butterfly, calendar spread, variance swap, VIX, term structure, forward volatility, model risk, early exercise, dividends, interest rates, put-call parity.

I. Volatility — The Core Variable

A. Two Volatilities, One Price

Table 1 — Historical vs Implied Volatility
TypeDefinitionUseDesk rule
Historical (HV)Realized std dev of log returns over a lookback window (typically 20–60d)Baseline; mean-reversion anchor20d HV for short-term; 60d for regime
Implied (IV)The σ that makes model price = market priceMarket's forward expectation + risk premiumIV > HV = fear premium; IV < HV = complacency
Natenberg's key insight: "Implied volatility is not a prediction of future volatility — it is the market price of volatility risk." The volatility risk premium (IV − RV) is persistently positive for index options; for single stocks it can flip.

B. Volatility Cone & Percentile Ranking

Natenberg advocates ranking current IV against its own history (IV rank / IV percentile):

Table 2 — IV Rank Interpretation
IV RankMeaningBias
> 80th pctlExpensive volNet seller (spreads, condors)
20–80thNormal rangeNeutral / directional
< 20th pctlCheap volNet buyer (long straddles, calendars)

II. The Greeks — Natenberg's Framework

Natenberg teaches Greeks as sensitivities to model inputs, not just risk metrics. Each Greek answers: "How much does my P&L change if this one thing moves?"

Table 3 — The Greeks (Natenberg's practical lens)
GreekMeasuresNatenberg's practical takeDesk cheat
Δ (Delta)∂P/∂S — directional exposure"Share equivalent." ATM ≈ 0.50; ITM → 1.0; OTM → 0. Dynamic, not static.Delta-hedge daily if |Δ| > 0.15 per contract
Γ (Gamma)∂Δ/∂S — delta instability"Convexity bill." Long gamma = pay theta to own convexity. Short gamma = collect theta, risk gap moves.Gamma scalping: re-hedge when Δ moves > 0.10
Θ (Theta)∂P/∂t — time decay"Rent on gamma." ATM decays fastest (√T). Θ ≈ −Γ·S²·σ²/2 (approx).Collect Θ via spreads/condors; never naked short near expiry
ν (Vega)∂P/∂σ — vol exposure"Vol beta." Long vega = long vol. Short vega = short vol. Vega peaks at ATM, drops at wings.Vega-neutral structures: calendar, ratio spreads
ρ (Rho)∂P/∂r — rate exposureUsually negligible for short-dated; matters for LEAPs and dividends.Ignore < 60 DTE; track for LEAPs
Natenberg's Gamma-Theta Trade-off: Γ·Θ ≈ −½·S²·σ²·Γ². You cannot collect theta without short gamma risk. Every short-theta position is a short-gamma position. The desk rule: define max gamma loss before entry.

III. Volatility Smile / Skew — The Market's Reality

Black-Scholes assumes constant σ. Reality: IV varies by strike (smile/skew) and expiry (term structure).

A. Skew Types

Table 4 — Skew Regimes
RegimeShapeCauseTrade implication
Equity skew (smirk)OTM puts > ATM > OTM callsCrash fear; leverage effect; put buying for insurancePut spreads cheaper than call spreads; risk reversals pay to sell
Commodity smileU-shaped: both wings > ATMSupply disruption risk both waysStraddles/strangles expensive; fly cheaper
FX smileSymmetric around ATMTwo-way tail riskRisk reversals ≈ 0; butterflies trade skew

B. Term Structure

IV vs DTE: usually upward sloping (longer dated = higher IV) due to uncertainty accumulation. Inverted during crisis (near-term panic > long-term).

Forward volatility: σfwd² = (σ₂²·T₂ − σ₁²·T₁) / (T₂ − T₁)
Use to price calendar spreads and forward-start options.

IV. Pricing Models — When to Use What

Table 5 — Model Selection
ModelUse caseLimitation

Black-76Futures optionsSame as BS
Binomial (Cox-Ross-Rubinstein)American exercise, discrete divs, flexibleSlow for many steps; converge to BS
TrinomialFaster convergence; better for barriersMore complex
Monte CarloPath-dependent (Asian, lookback, barrier)Slow; no Greeks without adjoint
Local Vol (Dupire)Fits full surface; prices exotics consistentlyOverfits; poor hedging (vol of vol = 0)
Stochastic Vol (Heston, SABR)Vol of vol, skew dynamics, term structureCalibration risk; parameters not observable
Natenberg's model warning: "All models are wrong. Some are useful." The desk uses BS for quoting conventions (IV is the language), binomial for American early exercise, and stochastic vol for structuring. Never trust a single model's absolute price.

V. Put-Call Parity — The Arbitrage Anchor

C − P = S − K·e−rT − PV(Dividends)

If violated → risk-free arb. Desk checks this every morning on core names. Violation = data error or hard-to-borrow.

VII. Volatility Trading Strategies — Natenberg's Playbook

A. Long Volatility (IV < RV expectation)

Long Straddle

ATM call + put. Max loss = premium. Needs large move > premium. Theta bleed daily.

Long Strangle

OTM call + put. Cheaper, needs bigger move. Wider breakevens.

Calendar Spread

Sell near-term, buy far-term same strike. Long vega, short theta. Wins if spot pins strike.

Diagonal Spread

Calendar + vertical. Directional + vol view.

B. Short Volatility (IV > RV expectation)

Short Straddle/Strangle

Collect premium. Unlimited risk. Desk never does naked.

Iron Condor

OTM put spread + OTM call spread. Defined risk. High prob, small premium.

Butterfly

Long 1 ITM + 2 ATM + 1 OTM (or all OTM). Pin risk. Cheap, high reward/risk.

Ratio Spread

Buy 1 ATM, sell 2 OTM. Vega-neutral near strike. Tail risk if blow through.

VIII. Risk Management — Natenberg's Discipline

The Natenberg Risk Rules (desk-adapted):
  1. Size to max loss, not max gain. Know the worst case before entry.
  2. Define exit before entry. Stop loss on vega? Delta? Time? All three.
  3. Vega limit per name. Net vega exposure capped at X% of capital.
  4. Gamma limit. Max short gamma = max daily move you can survive unhedged.
  5. No naked short gamma near earnings. Ever.
  6. IV rank context. Same structure = different risk at 90th vs 10th pctl IV.
  7. Model risk buffer. Price with 2 models; if they disagree > 5%, pass or size down.

IX. Quick Reference — Desk Formulas

ATM straddle price ≈ 0.8 × S × σ × √(T/252) (approx, for quick mental math) 1σ move ≈ S × σ × √(DTE/252) Theta (ATM) ≈ −0.4 × S × σ / √(T/252) per day Vega (ATM) ≈ 0.4 × S × √(T/252) per 1% vol change Gamma (ATM) ≈ 0.4 / (S × σ × √(T/252))

X. How the Desk Uses This

When a question hits the desk about options — any question — the answer is grounded in this framework:

  1. What is the IV rank? (Cheap/expensive vol)
  2. What is the skew telling us? (Crash fear? Complacency?)
  3. Which Greek is the primary risk? (Delta? Gamma? Vega? Theta?)
  4. What model fits the structure? (BS for quoting, binomial for early exercise, stochastic for exotics)
  5. Where is the edge vs the market's pricing?
  6. What is the max loss, and is it defined?
Carlton (or anyone) asks: "How do I trade earnings vol?" → Desk answers from Section VII: long straddle/strangle if IV rank low; iron condor/ratio spread if IV rank high; never naked short gamma through binary event. Every answer traces to a line in this page.
